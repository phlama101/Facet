## Section Outline

### Section 1 — Intro: "Volcanoes in the middle of nowhere"

**Hook:** The Big Island of Hawaii sits 3,200 km from the nearest plate boundary — in the middle of the Pacific Plate, far from any trench, ridge, or fault. Yet Kīlauea is one of Earth's most active volcanoes, erupting almost continuously. Look northwest along the Hawaiian chain and you see the pattern: Maui is older than the Big Island, Oʻahu is older than Maui, Kauaʻi is older still — and beyond Kauaʻi, the chain of islands continues as a chain of submerged seamounts stretching all the way to the Emperor Seamount Chain near the Aleutians, with seamounts up to 80 million years old. The islands are not random. They form a precisely age-progressive trail, stamped into the moving Pacific Plate by a fixed heat source in the mantle below: a mantle plume — a hotspot.

**Key Terms:**
- **Hotspot** — A long-lived zone of anomalously high volcanic activity, typically attributed to a mantle plume rising from deep in the mantle. Unlike boundary volcanism, hotspots are fixed relative to the moving plates above them, producing age-progressive chains of volcanic edifices.
- **Mantle plume** — A narrow, buoyant upwelling of anomalously hot mantle rock rising from depth (possibly the core-mantle boundary) through the overlying mantle. As the plume head rises and pressure decreases, decompression melting generates large volumes of basaltic magma.
- **Seamount chain** — A linear series of submarine volcanoes (seamounts) and volcanic islands produced as a tectonic plate moves over a fixed hotspot. Age increases systematically with distance from the active hotspot.
- **Flood basalt** — An exceptionally large outpouring of basaltic lava produced when the head of a newly arriving mantle plume melts catastrophically, covering vast areas in short geologic time. Also called a Large Igneous Province (LIP). Examples: Deccan Traps (India), Columbia River Basalt (Pacific NW).
- **Intraplate volcanism** — Volcanic activity occurring within the interior of a tectonic plate, far from any plate boundary. Hotspots are the primary cause of intraplate volcanism.

### Section 2 — Concept: "Fixed heat, moving plate"

**Body overview:**
- Mantle plume mechanics: hot, buoyant column rises from deep mantle; plume head reaches the base of the lithosphere; decompression melting generates basaltic magma; magma punches through the plate
- Why mostly basalt: no subducting slab → no water input → no water-fluxed melting → mafic primary melt (contrast with arc)
- Fixed plume + moving plate = age-progressive trail: oldest volcanic edifices furthest from active hotspot
- Hawaii-Emperor chain: Hawaiian chain trends NW (current Pacific Plate motion); Emperor chain trends N; 60° bend at ~50 Ma records abrupt change in Pacific Plate motion direction
- Using chain geometry to calculate plate speed: distance/age = velocity (~9 cm/yr for Pacific Plate)
- Iceland: sits on both the Mid-Atlantic Ridge AND a hotspot → double heat source → unusually thick and elevated oceanic crust, island above sea level
- Yellowstone: hotspot under thick continental crust; basaltic magma from plume heats and partially melts the overlying felsic crust → rhyolitic magma → explosive caldera eruptions; Snake River Plain = trail of ancient Yellowstone calderas as North America moved SW
- Flood basalts: plume heads on arrival produce massive LIPs — Deccan Traps (~66 Ma, India, possibly contributing to end-Cretaceous extinction); Columbia River Basalt (~17–6 Ma, PNW, predecessor of Yellowstone plume head)

### Section 3 — Quiz: 3 Questions

**Q1:** The Hawaiian Islands display a clear age progression: the Big Island is volcanically active today, while Kauaʻi (to the northwest) is geologically inactive and ~5 million years old. What process produces this pattern?
- A) The Hawaiian hotspot migrates slowly to the southeast over time, abandoning older islands and building new ones; the islands record the hotspot's path across the ocean floor
- B) A fixed mantle plume continuously supplies magma to the base of the Pacific Plate; as the plate moves to the northwest over the stationary plume, successive volcanoes form above it, each becoming inactive as the plate carries it away from the heat source ✓
- C) The Hawaiian Islands formed simultaneously at a mid-ocean ridge and then drifted apart as the Pacific Plate spread; the age difference reflects the time since each island separated from the ridge
- D) Periodic changes in Pacific Plate subduction rate cause pulses of intraplate volcanism; each island represents a separate pulse, with older pulses to the northwest because the subduction zone migrated in that direction

**Q2:** Hawaiian volcanoes erupt fluid basaltic lava in effusive eruptions, while Yellowstone produces explosive rhyolitic eruptions. Both are hotspots. What accounts for the dramatic difference in eruption style and magma composition?
- A) Hawaii has a more powerful mantle plume than Yellowstone; stronger plumes produce hotter, more fluid basaltic magma while weaker plumes produce cooler, more viscous rhyolite
- B) The Hawaiian plume rises beneath thin oceanic crust and erupts basaltic magma directly from the mantle with little crustal contamination; the Yellowstone plume rises beneath thick continental crust and its basaltic magma partially melts the surrounding felsic rock, generating silica-rich rhyolitic magma that is far more viscous and erupts explosively ✓
- C) Hawaii is at a younger stage of hotspot evolution — all hotspots begin with basaltic eruptions and eventually transition to rhyolitic as the magma chamber matures over millions of years
- D) The difference is due to water content: Hawaiian magma is dry because it travels through dry oceanic crust; Yellowstone magma absorbs water from continental rocks, which increases viscosity and drives explosive eruptions

**Q3:** The Hawaiian Island chain trends northwest, but northwest of Kauaʻi the chain of seamounts abruptly bends and trends nearly due north as the Emperor Seamount Chain. The bend occurs at seamounts dated to approximately 50 million years ago. What does this bend record?
- A) The Hawaiian mantle plume shifted position approximately 50 million years ago, changing the direction in which it supplied magma to the Pacific Plate above
- B) A change in Pacific Plate motion approximately 50 million years ago — the plate was previously moving roughly northward (building the Emperor chain) and then abruptly changed direction to the northwest (building the Hawaiian chain), with the bend in the seamount trail recording the timing and angle of the change ✓
- C) The bend is the result of two separate and unrelated hotspots — one responsible for the Emperor chain and a different one responsible for the Hawaiian chain — that happened to connect at the 50 Ma point
- D) Erosion by deep-ocean currents progressively shifted the positions of the seamounts over time, producing the apparent bend; the original chain was actually straight
