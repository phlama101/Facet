## Section Outline

### Section 1 — Intro: "A rock's life story"

**Hook:** A granite countertop on a kitchen island contains potassium feldspar crystals that grew slowly from magma 10 km underground, millions of years ago. That granite was eventually uplifted by tectonics, eroded grain by grain by rain and frost, transported as sand grains to a beach or riverbed, and could — given enough time — be buried, compressed into sandstone, and then metamorphosed into quartzite. The rock in your kitchen has a biography. The rock cycle is the framework for reading it.

**Key Terms:**
- **Rock** — A naturally occurring solid aggregate of one or more minerals (or, in some cases, mineral-like substances such as glass or organic material). The distinction from a mineral: a mineral has a fixed composition and crystal structure; a rock is an assemblage.
- **Rock cycle** — The continuous set of processes by which rocks of any family can be transformed into rocks of any other family through melting, crystallisation, weathering, erosion, deposition, burial, heat, and pressure. There is no obligatory starting point or fixed sequence.
- **Igneous rock** — Rock formed by the cooling and crystallisation of magma (molten rock). If cooling occurs underground, the result is intrusive (plutonic) igneous rock; if at the surface, extrusive (volcanic).
- **Sedimentary rock** — Rock formed at or near Earth's surface by the accumulation and lithification of sediment (fragments of pre-existing rocks or minerals, or precipitated minerals), or by the accumulation of organic material.
- **Metamorphic rock** — Rock formed when pre-existing rock is subjected to elevated temperature and/or pressure — conditions that alter the mineral assemblage and/or texture without fully melting the rock.

---

### Section 2 — Concept: "Three families, one cycle"

**Body overview:**
- What each rock family is and the process defining it (concise; detail deferred to 1.3.2–1.3.4)
- Why the cycle has no fixed starting point — any rock type can transform into any other given the right conditions
- Two energy sources: Earth's internal heat drives the igneous and metamorphic sides; solar energy (which drives the water and wind cycles) drives the sedimentary side
- Geologic time context: the cycle operates on timescales of millions to hundreds of millions of years, but the same processes are running right now

**Cards:**
| Card | Icon | Color | Content |
|------|------|-------|---------|
| Igneous Rocks | Flame | BRAND.ruby | Formed by cooling magma; intrusive (slow cooling underground → large crystals) vs. extrusive (fast cooling at surface → small crystals or glass). Examples: granite (intrusive), basalt (extrusive). |
| Sedimentary Rocks | Layers | BRAND.gold | Formed at surface by sediment accumulation and lithification, or by chemical precipitation. Examples: sandstone (clastic), limestone (chemical/biogenic), coal (organic). |
| Metamorphic Rocks | Thermometer | BRAND.amethyst | Formed by heat and pressure on pre-existing rock without melting. May develop foliation (parallel mineral alignment). Examples: marble (from limestone), quartzite (from sandstone), schist (from shale). |

### Section 3 — Quiz: 3 Questions

**Q1:** Which process is responsible for forming igneous rocks?
- A) Burial and compaction of loose sediment particles
- B) Cooling and crystallisation of molten rock (magma or lava)  ✓
- C) Application of heat and pressure to pre-existing solid rock
- D) Chemical precipitation of dissolved minerals from seawater

**Q2:** The rock cycle is powered by two distinct energy sources. Which pair correctly identifies them?
- A) Solar radiation and gravitational collapse of Earth's core
- B) Earth's internal heat (from radioactive decay and primordial heat) and solar energy (which drives weather, erosion, and deposition)  ✓
- C) Tidal energy from the Moon and geothermal energy from volcanoes only
- D) Chemical energy stored in minerals and kinetic energy from tectonic plate movement

**Q3:** A geologist finds a rock that was originally shale. It has been buried at depth, exposed to elevated temperature and pressure, and now shows a strong planar fabric with aligned minerals. Which rock family does it now belong to, and why?
- A) Sedimentary — burial compacts sediment into rock, and shale is already a sedimentary rock, so it remains in that family
- B) Igneous — elevated temperatures at depth cause the minerals to melt and recrystallise as an igneous rock
- C) Metamorphic — heat and pressure altered the mineral assemblage and texture of the pre-existing rock without fully melting it, producing a new rock in the metamorphic family  ✓
- D) It cannot be classified — a rock that began as sedimentary but was later buried does not fit any single family
