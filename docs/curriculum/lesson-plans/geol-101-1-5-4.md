## Learning Objectives

2. Describe the major processes shaping coastlines — wave erosion, longshore drift, sea-level change — and the landforms they produce.
3. Distinguish erosional coasts (cliffs, sea stacks, arches) from depositional coasts (beaches, barrier islands, spits), and glacial erosional landforms (cirques, arêtes, U-valleys, fjords) from depositional landforms (moraines, drumlins, kettles).

## Section Outline

### Section 1 — Intro: "Ice and ocean: the last great sculptors"
**Hook:** During the Last Glacial Maximum (~20,000 years ago), ice sheets covered ~30% of Earth's land surface. Global sea level was ~120 m lower than today — the English Channel was dry land, and mammoths roamed what is now the North Sea floor. Today's landscapes in northern Europe, Canada, and New Zealand still bear the unmistakable imprint of that vanished ice. Meanwhile, at every ocean margin, waves relentlessly attack rock and redistribute sediment — building beaches, barrier islands, and spits while simultaneously eroding cliffs and sea stacks. Ice and ocean are the last two great sculptors of Earth's surface at the scale of human civilisation.

**Key terms:** glacier, glacial erosion, moraine, fjord, wave base, longshore drift, barrier island.

### Section 2 — Concept: "How ice and waves reshape the land"

#### Glacial Processes and Landforms
- **Glacial erosion**: two mechanisms — *abrasion* (rock debris embedded in basal ice grinds bedrock like sandpaper → striae, polished surfaces) and *plucking/quarrying* (meltwater freezes in cracks, ice pulls rock blocks free)
- Alpine (valley) glaciers:
  - **Cirque**: armchair-shaped hollow at glacier head, where ice accumulates (Cwm, corrie)
  - **Arête**: knife-edged ridge between two cirques
  - **Horn**: pyramidal peak where three or more cirques converge (Matterhorn)
  - **U-shaped valley**: glacier widens and deepens river V-valley into characteristic U-shape (Yosemite Valley, Milford Sound)
  - **Hanging valley**: tributary glacier meets main glacier at elevation → tributary valley left high above main valley floor → waterfall (Bridalveil Fall, Yosemite)
  - **Fjord**: glacially eroded U-valley flooded by sea-level rise (Norway, New Zealand, Chile, Alaska)
- Continental (ice sheet) glaciers:
  - Cover entire landscapes — Canada, Scandinavia, Antarctica; erode bedrock on a massive scale
  - Produce **roche moutonnée**: asymmetric bedrock knobs (smooth upstream stoss side, plucked downstream lee side)
  - **Drumlins**: streamlined oval hills of glacial sediment, formed under active ice — point in direction of ice flow
- **Glacial deposition**: till (unsorted, unstratified debris deposited directly from ice) vs. outwash (sorted, stratified sand/gravel deposited by meltwater streams)
  - **Moraine**: ridge of till — terminal moraine (marking farthest ice advance), lateral moraine (along ice margin), medial moraine (two lateral moraines merging)
  - **Kettle lakes**: depressions left by melting buried ice blocks, filled with water (Minnesota's 10,000 lakes)
  - **Eskers**: sinuous ridges of sand and gravel deposited by subglacial meltwater streams
- **Ice ages and sea level**: during glacial maxima, water locked in ice sheets lowers global sea level by up to 120 m; during deglaciation, meltwater raises sea level, flooding coastlines and creating new continental shelves and fjords; the 20,000-year sea-level record is one of geology's most powerful tools for reading past climate

#### Coastal Processes and Landforms
- **Wave energy**: waves transfer energy from wind to ocean surface; waves break when water depth < ½ wavelength (wave base); breakers erode coastlines by hydraulic action (wave pressure), abrasion (suspended sediment), and solution
- **Erosional coastal landforms** — rocky, high-energy coasts:
  - **Sea cliff**: steep erosional face where wave energy cuts into rock
  - **Wave-cut notch and platform**: notch at base of cliff, horizontal platform extending seaward at low-tide level
  - **Sea arch**: headland with cave on both sides, cave roofs erode through → arch
  - **Sea stack**: arch collapses → isolated column of rock offshore (Old Man of Hoy, Scotland)
- **Longshore drift**: waves approach coast at an angle → swash carries sediment obliquely up beach → backwash drags sediment straight back by gravity → net transport of sediment along coast; the dominant process distributing sediment along continental coastlines
- **Depositional coastal landforms** — low-energy or sediment-rich coasts:
  - **Beach**: accumulation of loose sediment (sand, gravel) in dynamic equilibrium between wave energy and sediment supply
  - **Spit**: sand bar extending from headland across a bay mouth, fed by longshore drift; may curve at tip (recurved spit)
  - **Barrier island**: long, low-lying sand island parallel to coast, separated from mainland by lagoon; very common on US East Coast and Gulf Coast; formed by longshore drift and wave reworking of shelf sediments
  - **Lagoon**: shallow, calm water body protected behind barrier island or spit; accumulates fine sediment and organic matter
- **Sea-level change**: long-term sea-level change (glacial/interglacial cycles) controls whether coasts are transgressive (sea advancing inland — drowned river mouths = rias, fjords) or regressive (sea retreating — exposed coastal plains, raised beaches); current anthropogenic sea-level rise (~3.7 mm/yr and accelerating) threatens barrier islands, deltas, and low-lying coastal cities
