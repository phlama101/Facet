## Learning Objectives

2. Explain how rivers erode, transport, and deposit sediment, and what landforms each process creates.
3. Identify key fluvial landforms: V-valleys, canyons, floodplains, meanders, oxbow lakes, deltas, alluvial fans.

## Section Outline

### Section 1 — Intro: "The river as sculptor"
**Hook:** The Colorado River carved the Grand Canyon — 1.6 km deep, 446 km long — by eroding through 1.8 billion years of rock in roughly 5–6 million years. Every landscape feature in the canyon is a layer in a stratigraphic column you can read from rim to river. Rivers are Earth's most powerful surface sculptors over geologic time.
**Key terms:** drainage basin, base level, stream gradient, floodplain, alluvial fan.

### Section 2 — Concept: "From mountain to sea"
- Drainage basins and the watershed concept
- Stream gradient: steep headwaters (erosion dominant) → gentle lower reaches (deposition dominant)
- Base level: rivers erode toward sea level; changes in base level (sea level rise/fall, dam construction) reshape the whole system
- Erosional landforms: V-valleys (steep gradient, active downcutting), canyons (incised meanders or rapid uplift), waterfalls (resistant rock layer)
- Transport: bedload, suspended load, dissolved load; competence and capacity
- Depositional landforms: floodplains (lateral migration + overbank deposition), meanders (inside bend = point bar; outside bend = cut bank), oxbow lakes (meander cutoff), deltas (river meets standing water — Mississippi, Nile), alluvial fans (steep canyon exits onto flat plain — Death Valley)
- Cards: Erosional River Landforms (Waves, BRAND.accent), Depositional River Landforms (Layers, BRAND.gold), River Systems (Globe, BRAND.jade)

### Section 3 — Quiz: 3 Questions
1. Why do rivers erode in their upper reaches but deposit in their lower reaches?
2. How does a meander form, migrate, and eventually produce an oxbow lake?
3. What is base level and how does a change in base level (e.g. dam, sea level fall) affect an entire river system?
