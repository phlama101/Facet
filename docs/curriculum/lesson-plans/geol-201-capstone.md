# Lesson Plan: GEOL 201 — Final Exam (Capstone)

## Metadata
- **Course:** GEOL 201 — Earth Through Time
- **Module:** Capstone
- **Lesson:** GEOL 201 Final Exam
- **Level:** Intermediate (200-level)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **XP Reward:** 500
- **Track:** Geology (geo)
- **Prerequisites:** All of GEOL 201 (Modules 2.1–2.5)

---

## Learning Objectives

1. Apply relative and absolute dating methods to construct a sequence of geological events from a described stratigraphic or structural scenario.
2. Interpret sedimentary facies and stratigraphic sequences in terms of depositional environments and sea-level change.
3. Use fossil evidence, trace fossils, and biostratigraphic principles to constrain the age and environment of deposition of a rock unit.
4. Identify the major evolutionary events of the Phanerozoic (Cambrian explosion, mass extinctions, conquest of land, Cenozoic radiation) and place them in temporal and causal context.
5. Apply field methods — geological map reading, structural geology, stratigraphic section measurement, and rock/mineral identification — to interpret an integrated geological scenario.

---

## Scope Boundaries

**Covers:** Integrated synthesis across all GEOL 201 modules: geological timescale and dating methods (2.1); stratigraphy and the rock record (2.2); the fossil record (2.3); major events in Earth history (2.4); field methods (2.5). Cross-cutting integration of multiple skill sets is expected in every question.
**Does not cover:** Material exclusive to GEOL 101; quantitative geochemistry beyond the level taught; advanced structural analysis (stereonets, balanced cross-sections); thin-section petrography; detailed palaeoclimate modelling.

## Section Outline

### Section 1 — Intro: "Putting it all together"
**Hook:** A geological field campaign doesn't separate stratigraphy from palaeontology from structural geology from geochronology. You stand at an outcrop and everything arrives at once: the structural orientation of the beds, the grain size and sedimentary structures, the fossils, the contacts — and from all of it together you build the story of what happened, in what order, and why. That synthesis is what this exam tests. Each question reaches across modules and asks you to combine what you know about time, strata, life, history, and field methods into a coherent geological interpretation.

### Section 2 — Quiz: 10 Questions

**Q1 (Integration: 2.1 + 2.2).** A geologist describes three rock units exposed in a cliff face. The bottom unit (A) is a limestone with abundant trilobites; the middle unit (B) is a sandstone that truncates A at an irregular, incised surface; the top unit (C) is a shale with ammonites. A radiometric date from a volcanic ash layer immediately above unit B gives 350 Ma. What is the relative age relationship of these three units, what type of contact separates A from B, and what geological period does 350 Ma correspond to?

**Q2 (Integration: 2.1 + 2.3).** You recover conodonts from a limestone sample, and they belong to a conodont biozone that existed only from 443 to 419 Ma. A radiometric date from an igneous dyke that cuts the limestone gives 430 ± 5 Ma. Is the dyke intrusion older or younger than the limestone? What does the biozone age tell you about the limestone? Are these two constraints consistent, and how do they compare to what the index fossil age implies for the dyke?

**Q3 (Integration: 2.2 + 2.3).** You observe a sequence of beds from base to top: (i) black, laminated shale with no fossils; (ii) grey shale with rare, thin-shelled bivalves; (iii) limestone with diverse corals, brachiopods, and echinoderms; (iv) oolitic limestone. Describe the transgressive or regressive nature of this sequence, the direction of sea-level change, and explain what the fossil diversity trend tells you about water depth and oxygen levels across the sequence.

**Q4 (Integration: 2.2 + 2.5).** A geological map shows a sandstone formation that, in two adjacent river valleys, forms contacts that V upstream (northward) in one valley draining south and V downstream (southward) in the other valley draining north. What does this pattern tell you about the dip of the sandstone? Both valleys drain in opposite directions from a ridge — what structural feature might this ridge represent?

**Q5 (Integration: 2.3 + 2.4).** The Burgess Shale (508 Ma, Cambrian) and the Chengjiang Lagerstätte (518 Ma, Cambrian) both preserve exceptional soft-bodied organism assemblages. Why do soft tissues preserve in these deposits but not in typical marine sedimentary sequences? What do these faunas tell us about the Cambrian Explosion, and which major animal body plans first appear in the Cambrian fossil record?

**Q6 (Integration: 2.4 + 2.1).** The end-Permian mass extinction (~252 Ma) eliminated ~96% of marine species. Describe two independent lines of geological evidence that document this event in the rock record, name the primary kill mechanism that is best supported by evidence, and explain why recovery of marine ecosystems took approximately 10 million years.

**Q7 (Integration: 2.4 + 2.5).** A volcanic rock contains ~4 mm plagioclase and hornblende phenocrysts in a grey aphanitic groundmass, with vesicles now filled by calcite. A TimeScale geological map shows this rock sitting unconformably over folded Devonian sedimentary rocks, with the unconformity surface showing 10 m of relief. Describe: (a) the rock type; (b) what the texture tells you about eruption; (c) what the calcite amygdales indicate; (d) what the unconformity beneath tells you about the geological history of the area before this eruption.

**Q8 (Integration: 2.2 + 2.5).** You measure a 15 m stratigraphic section using a Jacob's staff. From the base up you record: (i) 3 m of conglomerate with erosive base; (ii) 6 m of cross-bedded sandstone fining upward; (iii) 4 m of siltstone with ripple lamination and gradational top; (iv) 2 m of mudstone with desiccation cracks and root traces. Describe the depositional environment of each unit, construct a brief verbal description of the facies model, and explain how each contact type (erosive, gradational) supports your interpretation.

**Q9 (Integration: 2.3 + 2.4).** The Permo-Triassic boundary is identified in marine sections worldwide by: (1) the disappearance of most marine invertebrate taxa; (2) a negative carbon isotope excursion in carbonate sediments; (3) the appearance of microbial carbonates (stromatolites) that had been suppressed by grazing invertebrates for hundreds of millions of years. Explain what each of these three lines of evidence tells you about conditions during and after the end-Permian extinction, and how they are consistent with massive volcanic carbon release (Siberian Traps) as the primary driver.

**Q10 (Integration: All modules).** You are mapping a mountainous terrain. You observe: (a) folded Ordovician–Silurian sedimentary rocks with well-developed axial planar cleavage; (b) an angular unconformity overlain by undeformed Carboniferous conglomerates and sandstones; (c) a thrust fault cutting the Carboniferous strata, placing Ordovician rocks over Carboniferous; (d) a pluton of granite intruding the thrust sheet; (e) the granite gives a U-Pb zircon age of 305 Ma. Construct a complete sequence of geological events (from oldest to youngest) for this area, giving a reason for each inference.
